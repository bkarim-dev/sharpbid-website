How we measure
miscellaneous metals

Misc metals is measured off details and schedules, with the architectural and structural sets read together. Almost every scope gap in this trade comes from reading only one of the two — the rail is on the A-sheets, the embed that holds it is on the S-sheets.

DETAIL NUMBER

Every item is tied to the detail it comes from

Rails, stairs and fabrications are grouped by detail number, not by location, because that's how a shop estimates and how a fabrication drawing gets produced. The workbook reads like a cut list with quantities, and every line points back at a drawing you can open.

TWO SETS

Architectural and structural read against each other

The rail is on the A-sheets and its embed is on the S-sheets. Lintels are scheduled structurally and shown architecturally. Equipment supports come from the mechanical layout. We read all of them together, and where they disagree, both readings go on the assumptions page.

PER FLIGHT

Stairs are broken down, never counted as stairs

A stair is stringers, treads, risers, landings, handrails and guards — different material, different shop time, different install. Counting "one stair" hides all of it. We report per flight with the components separated, so shop hours attach to the right item.

FINISH

Galvanizing and finish recorded per item

Hot-dip galvanizing changes cost, adds a trip and adds lead time. Shop primer, field paint and architectural finishes all price differently again. Finish is written against every line so procurement and schedule both work off the same list.

CONDITIONS

Rails measured by condition, not by total length

Wall-mounted, floor-mounted, fascia-mounted, guard versus handrail, sloped versus level — each is a different rate over the same lineal foot. Post counts come from spacing in the detail rather than from an assumed average.

EXCLUSIONS BY LIST

"Miscellaneous metals as required" is answered with a list

Specs love that phrase, and it means everything nobody else claimed. We give you an itemized list of what was measured, so your exclusions can be specific instead of a blanket qualification that a general contractor will read past.

COORDINATION

Embeds tied to the pour they go in

Embeds are counted by type against the element they're cast into, which is what makes them useful on site rather than just in a bid. It's the same reading that drives the concrete and formwork takeoff from the other side.

UNITS

Metric or imperial, whichever you bid in

Commercial and institutional sets in Canada are commonly metric; residential work is often imperial. The workbook arrives in the units you price with, or both columns, with detail numbers and marks carried through exactly as drawn.

Scope gaps we flag
on Div 05 packages

The six that cost metals fabricators money most often. Each one lands on the assumptions and exclusions page with the sheet it came from, so you can qualify it in your bid instead of meeting it in a change order.

A-SHEETS VS S-SHEETS

Embeds live on the structural set

The rail lives on the architectural sheets, the embed that holds it lives on the structural. Bid one set and half the package is somebody's assumption — and embeds have to be on site before the pour, so the discovery is expensive as well as late.

SPEC LANGUAGE

"Miscellaneous metals as required"

It means everything no other trade claimed, and it's a blank cheque unless you answer it with a list. Exclude by itemized list, not by hope — a general qualification is worth very little in a scope meeting.

M-SHEETS

Equipment supports and dunnage

Rooftop units, tanks and mechanical equipment need steel supports drawn on the mechanical or structural sheets. The equipment belongs to another trade; the steel under it is Division 05, and it's rarely on any architectural drawing.

FINISH

Galvanizing assumed rather than scheduled

Whether an item is hot-dip galvanized, shop primed or architecturally finished is a spec decision with real cost and real lead time attached. Assumed wrong on a large rail package, it's a margin problem you can't recover mid-fabrication.

DIVISION LINE

Glass guards that might be Division 08

Glass railings and guards sit in Division 05 on some specs and Division 08 on others. We name which one the spec uses so you and the glazing sub aren't both excluding it — or both carrying it.

SCHEDULES

Lintels scheduled but not shown

Lintel schedules sit on the structural sheets and rarely repeat on the architectural. Every masonry and concrete opening has one, they're heavy, they're usually galvanized, and they're easy to miss when the count is taken off elevations.

Misc metals takeoff FAQs

How much does a misc metals takeoff cost?
From $600. Division 05 sits above the finish-trade tier because the details, schedules and structural sheets all have to be read together. What moves the fixed price is how many distinct details there are, the number of levels, and whether the package includes stairs. A written fixed quote comes back within 4 business hours. Full schedule on the takeoff pricing page.
How fast is it delivered?
Every Division 05 set gets a firm delivery date with the fixed quote, sized to the number of details. Same-day rush is +50%. If it lands after the date on the quote, you pay half.
Do you read the structural drawings as well as the architectural?
Yes, and on this trade it's the whole job. Embeds, weld plates, lintel schedules and equipment dunnage are drawn structurally while the item they serve is drawn architecturally. A takeoff built from one set only is missing half of Division 05 by definition.
How are stairs presented?
Per flight, with stringers, treads, risers, landings, handrails and guards as separate lines. Shop hours and material both attach to components, not to the word "stair", so that's how the workbook reports them.
Can you give me a list I can hand to the shop?
That's the format. Items are grouped by detail number with finish, size and quantity against each, which reads as a fabrication list rather than a bid summary. Add your own cost codes and it drops straight into your system.
What drawings do you need?
Architectural plans, sections and details; the structural set including lintel and embed schedules; and the mechanical roof and equipment layout if dunnage is in your scope. What's excluded?
Pricing, unless you add price placeholders. Structural steel framing is a separate scope from miscellaneous metals — tell us where your line sits and we'll respect it. Mechanical and electrical entirely; SharpBid doesn't take off Divisions 21 through 28, though supports for that equipment are counted where they're Division 05.
