How we measure
flooring

The finish schedule is the spine, and the plan tags are the check. Every room gets matched against both before a square foot is counted — because on most sets the two disagree somewhere, and whichever one you priced, the other one shows up later.

RECONCILE FIRST

Finish schedule against plan tags, room by room

Rooms are measured individually and tied to a finish code. Where the schedule and the plan tag disagree, both are listed on the assumptions page with the one we measured. You bid a known position instead of inheriting somebody's typo.

NET AREA

Measured to the face of finish, with deductions stated

Areas are taken to the finished face of wall. Casework, millwork bases, columns and floor-mounted equipment are deducted or not according to a rule written on the assumptions page — the same rule everywhere in the workbook, so your unit rates land on a consistent basis.

ROLL VS TILE

Roll goods and modular tile are different problems

Broadloom and sheet vinyl are governed by roll width and seam layout, not just area; carpet tile and LVT are area plus pattern. We keep them in separate sections so your material buy reflects how each one actually cuts.

PATTERN

Direction and pattern are called out, not averaged

Quarter-turn versus monolithic, plank direction, ashlar and herringbone all change what you buy over the same square footage. Where the spec or the finish plan sets a pattern, it's recorded against the area so your waste allowance is a decision instead of a guess.

EDGES

Base and transitions by condition, not by door count

Base is measured off the room perimeter net of openings, by finish type, and switches where the adjacent floor finish switches — tile base in wet areas, rubber elsewhere. Transitions are counted by the condition that actually occurs at each junction, which is rarely the same number as the doors.

MOISTURE

Conditional mitigation gets quantified anyway

Specs usually make mitigation conditional on a test result that doesn't exist at bid time. We measure the slab areas it could apply to and flag it as an allowance with a number attached, so you can qualify a quantity rather than carry the whole slab.

WASTE

Waste is stated, never buried in the quantity

Measured quantities are net. Waste is listed separately per material on the assumptions page so you can apply your own figures — your crews, your seam layout, your supplier's roll width. A takeoff that hides waste inside the number is a takeoff you can't check.

UNITS

Metric or imperial, whichever you bid in

Commercial and institutional sets in Canada are commonly metric; residential work is often imperial. The workbook arrives in the units you price with, or both columns, and finish codes stay exactly as the schedule writes them.

GAPSScope gaps we flagCaught before you bid

Scope gaps we flag
on flooring packages

The six that cost flooring subs money most often. Each one lands on the assumptions and exclusions page with the sheet it came from, so you can qualify it in your bid instead of meeting it in a change order.

SCHEDULE VS PLAN

The finish schedule and the plan tag disagree

The schedule says LVT-1, the plan tag says CPT-1. Whichever you priced, the other one turns up in a change order — against you. We list the conflict, the room and the sheet, so it gets resolved in an RFI instead of on site.

OPEN ALLOWANCE

Moisture mitigation with no quantity

"Mitigation where relative humidity exceeds the manufacturer's limit" is an open allowance with your name on it. Qualify a square footage or you own the whole slab, and on a slab-on-grade podium that is a very large number.

SPEC, NOT SCHEDULE

Floor prep that isn't on any drawing

Levelling, grinding, patching and the flatness tolerance the finish demands live in the Division 09 spec and the existing-conditions notes, not the finish schedule. On tenant improvement work over an old slab this is often the single biggest variable in the bid.

JUNCTIONS

Transitions counted per door

Transitions happen wherever two finishes meet — open-plan boundaries, corridor edges, thresholds without doors. Counting one per door leaf undercounts profiles badly on any plan with open transitions, and profiles are cheap material with real labour attached.

DIVISION LINE

Entrance matting frames that aren't Division 09

Recessed entrance grids are often specified in Division 12 with the frame supplied by someone else and the recess formed by the concrete trade. We name which division the spec puts each part in, so you're not quoting a frame you were never going to supply — or leaving one out.

WET AREAS

Waterproofing upturns measured off the details

Washroom and shower waterproofing membrane doesn't stop at the floor. Curbs, upturns at walls and the height it runs to are in the details, not the plan, and pricing the flat area alone leaves real material and real labour out of your number.

Flooring takeoff FAQs

How much does a flooring takeoff cost?
From $450 — the single-trade finish takeoff tier. What moves the fixed price is sheet count, how many finish codes are in the schedule, how many levels there are, and how much of the set genuinely repeats. A written fixed quote comes back within 4 business hours and doesn't move afterwards. Full schedule on the takeoff pricing page.
How fast is it delivered?
Next business day on single-trade flooring sets. Larger or multi-building sets get a firm delivery date with the quote. Same-day rush is +50%. If it lands after the date on the quote, you pay half.
Do you add waste factors to the quantities?
No — quantities are net, and waste is stated separately per material on the assumptions page. Your seam layout, your supplier's roll width and your crews' habits decide the right figure, and none of that belongs hidden inside someone else's square footage. If you'd rather the workbook apply your own percentages, send them and we'll build the column in.
How do I send drawings, and what formats do you accept?
Send a link — Dropbox, Drive, WeTransfer, BuildingConnected, BidCentral, SmartBid, or a GC plan room login. PDF is ideal and vector PDFs measure fastest; scanned sets are fine with a scale bar or a known dimension. Send the finish schedule and the spec section if they're separate from the drawing set.
Can the workbook use our own cost codes?
Yes. CSI MasterFormat is the default; send your cost code list and the workbook is built to it, so quantities drop into your estimating system without re-mapping.
How do you handle moisture mitigation when the spec is conditional?
We measure the slab areas the requirement could apply to and present it as a quantified allowance rather than leaving it open. You then qualify a square footage in your bid instead of carrying an undefined exposure across the whole floor plate.
What's excluded?
Pricing, unless you add price placeholders. Mechanical and electrical entirely — SharpBid doesn't take off Divisions 21 through 28. Anything the drawings genuinely leave undetermined is listed on the assumptions page instead of buried in a quantity.
