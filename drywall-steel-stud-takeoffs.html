How we measure
drywall and steel stud

We measure from the partition type legend, not the floor plan. Every wall carries a type mark before a square foot is counted, and any wall the drawings leave untagged is flagged rather than quietly assumed into the nearest type.

TYPE MARK FIRST

The legend drives the workbook

Partition types come off the legend, with gauge, depth, spacing, layers per side, board type, insulation and rating recorded for each. Areas are measured against that list — the difference between a takeoff you can check against the spec and a pile of square footage.

LAYERS ARE NOT AREA

Board and framing are counted separately

A double-layer-both-sides partition carries four times the board of a single-layer-one-side wall and exactly the same framing. Board goes in by layer, framing by lineal foot, finishing by the area actually taped. One blended rate over one number prices all three wrong.

HEIGHT

Wall height comes from the sections, not the plan

Two identical plan lengths at different heights are different line items. Terminations come from the sections, the RCPs and the wall types: slab-to-slab, to underside of deck or structure, or dying off above the ceiling. Where the set contradicts itself, both readings go on the assumptions page with the one we used.

HEAD OF WALL

Deflection is read out of the structural set

Anticipated deflection, slotted track and slip-clip requirements usually sit in the structural drawings and general notes, not on the architectural partition types. Rated head-of-wall assemblies carry firestop and their own top-track detail, so they get their own lineal-foot line.

SHAFTWALL

Rated shaftwall never sits with standard partitions

Shaftwall and area separation assemblies look identical on plan and cost nothing like a stud partition to build. They're reported separately with the rating attached, so your labour reflects the assembly and not the outline.

CEILINGS

Bulkheads are measured as developed area

A soffit has a vertical face and a horizontal underside. Both get measured, with the lineal run alongside for framing. Where a bulkhead meets an acoustic ceiling, the division of work is flagged — see acoustic ceiling takeoffs for the other half of that scope.

REPETITION

Typical floors are measured once and extended, visibly

A typical floor is measured in full and extended across the levels it applies to. The workbook shows which levels were measured and which were extended; where a level differs it gets measured on its own. You can audit the multiplier.

UNITS

Metric or imperial, whichever you bid in

Commercial and institutional sets in Canada are commonly metric; residential work is often imperial. The workbook arrives in the units you price with, or both columns. Opening deductions follow a rule written on the assumptions page, not an unwritten habit.

GAPSScope gaps we flagCaught before you bid

Scope gaps we flag
on drywall packages

The six that cost drywall subs money most often. Each one lands on the assumptions and exclusions page with the sheet it came from, so you can qualify it in your bid instead of meeting it in a change order.

S-SHEETS

Deflection hiding in the structural set

Deflection requirements are routinely specified in the structural general notes and never repeated on the architectural partition types. Price top track off the architectural alone and every deflection head is wrong — every wall, every floor.

RATINGS

Shaftwall counted as standard partition

Same square footage on plan, different assembly, roughly twice the labour. Elevator shafts, stair enclosures and service risers are where it hides. Type marks first, areas second — that ordering is the whole defence.

INTERIOR ELEVATIONS

Backing and blocking that isn't on any plan

Grab bars, accessories, millwork, wall-mounted TVs, handrails and equipment all need backing, and none of it shows on a floor plan. It lives in interior elevations and the accessory and equipment schedules. Left out, it's unpriced labour at the worst point in the job.

RCP

Bulkhead framing on the wrong side of the line

Where an acoustic ceiling dies into a drywall bulkhead, the framing can land with either trade depending on how the spec is written. We flag the condition and the lineal quantity so you price it or exclude it deliberately.

SPEC, NOT SCHEDULE

Level 5 finish written into the specification

Level of finish is a Division 09 spec item, not a finish schedule item. Lobbies, feature walls and anything under raking light often call for Level 5 while the schedule says nothing. Different rate, real area, easy to bid at Level 4 by default.

DIVISION LINE

Exterior sheathing that may not be yours

Glass-mat exterior sheathing sits in Division 09 on some projects and with the framer or the envelope trade on others. We measure it, keep it in its own section, and name the division the spec puts it in — so you include or exclude it on purpose. Same question in roofing and envelope takeoffs.

FAQDrywall takeoff questions

Drywall takeoff FAQs

How much does a drywall takeoff cost?
From $450 — the same tier as the other single-trade finish takeoffs. What moves the fixed price is sheet count, partition types in the legend, number of levels, and how much of the set genuinely repeats. A written fixed quote comes back within 4 business hours and doesn't move afterwards. Full schedule on the takeoff pricing page.
How fast is it delivered?
Next business day on single-trade drywall sets. Larger or multi-building sets get a firm delivery date with the quote. Same-day rush is +50%. If it lands after the date on the quote, you pay half — that's the whole guarantee.
Do you separate framing from board?
Yes, always — they carry different labour rates and different suppliers. A double-layer-both-sides partition has four times the board of a single-layer-one-side wall and identical framing, so one blended square-foot rate prices both wrong.
How do I send drawings, and what formats do you accept?
Send a link — Dropbox, Drive, WeTransfer, BuildingConnected, BidCentral, SmartBid, or a GC plan room login. PDF is ideal and vector PDFs measure fastest; scanned sets are fine with a scale bar or a known dimension. If all you have is the architectural, say so — we'll tell you what the structural set would change.
Can the workbook use our own cost codes?
Yes. CSI MasterFormat is the default; send your cost code list and the workbook is built to it, so quantities drop into your estimating system without re-mapping.
What's excluded?
Pricing, unless you add price placeholders. Mechanical and electrical entirely — SharpBid doesn't take off Divisions 21 through 28. Anything the drawings genuinely leave undetermined is listed on the assumptions page instead of buried in a quantity.
What happens when an addendum lands mid-bid?
Addenda during the bid period are part of the service, and one revision round is included. If an addendum changes scope enough to move the delivery date, the new date is confirmed in writing — so the guarantee still means something.
