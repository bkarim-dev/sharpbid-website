How we measure
painting and coatings

We measure from the painting schedule in the specification, not the room finish schedule. The room finish schedule tells you a wall is painted; the spec tells you which system, how many coats and what preparation — and that is what your rate is actually built on.

SYSTEM FIRST

The spec's coating schedule drives the workbook

Each surface is matched to its system — substrate, primer, number of finish coats, sheen and preparation. Areas are then measured against that list. Two walls with the same square footage and different systems are two lines, because they're two different costs.

SUBSTRATE

Substrate is never averaged

CMU takes block filler and soaks up material at a completely different rate from gypsum board. Concrete, plaster and wood each behave differently again. Substrate is recorded per area so material and labour both track what's actually being coated.

HEIGHT

Wall height and access come off the sections

Painted height follows the wall, not the ceiling — a wall carried to underside of deck above an open ceiling is painted well past the visible line. High work, atriums, stairwells and exterior elevations are separated so staging and access can be priced against real areas instead of an average.

DOORS

Doors and frames counted from the door schedule

Whether a door is site-painted or pre-finished is decided in the Division 08 door schedule, not in the Division 09 finish plan. We count leaves and sides from the door schedule and mark which ones the spec hands to the factory, so you don't price a finish that was never yours.

RCP

Exposed deck is a reflected ceiling plan item

Spray-painted exposed structure almost never appears on the room finish schedule. It's called up on the reflected ceiling plans and in the spec, and it is one of the largest single areas on an open-ceiling project. We take it off the RCPs level by level.

DEDUCTIONS

Openings and unpainted surfaces follow one stated rule

Glazing, millwork faces, wall protection and anything factory-finished are deducted or not according to a rule written on the assumptions page and applied identically everywhere. Consistency is what lets your unit rate mean the same thing on every line.

SPECIALTY

High-performance systems stay in their own sections

Epoxy, urethane, intumescent and traffic coatings carry different material costs, different applicators and often different warranty obligations. Folding them into a general wall area is the fastest way to lose money on a job you won.

UNITS

Metric or imperial, whichever you bid in

Commercial and institutional sets in Canada are commonly metric; residential work is often imperial. The workbook arrives in the units you price with, or both columns, with the spec's own system codes carried through unchanged.

GAPSScope gaps we flagCaught before you bid

Scope gaps we flag
on painting packages

The six that cost painting subs money most often. Each one lands on the assumptions and exclusions page with the sheet it came from, so you can qualify it in your bid instead of meeting it in a change order.

DIV 08, NOT DIV 09

Site-painted versus pre-finished doors

That decision is made in the Division 08 door schedule and never repeated in Division 09. Count doors off the finish plan and you'll either price factory-finished leaves you were never supplying, or miss a few hundred sides that were always yours.

RCP

Exposed deck spray that's on no finish schedule

On an open-ceiling project the deck, joists and hangers can be the largest painted area in the building, and it appears only on the reflected ceiling plans. Miss it and you've missed a level's worth of work in a single line.

SUBSTRATE

Block filler treated as primer

CMU needs block filler before anything else goes on, and it consumes material at a rate nothing like a sealer on drywall. Priced as a coat of primer over the same square footage, a large block wall quietly eats the margin on the whole package.

SCHEDULES

Mechanical and electrical items scheduled to be painted

Exposed conduit, ductwork, grilles, diffusers and bollards routinely carry a "paint to match" note in a schedule that isn't yours to read. The equipment belongs to another trade; the coating on it belongs to you.

PREP

Surface preparation written only in the spec

Level of finish on the drywall, patching of existing surfaces, and the cleaning or profiling a high-performance system demands are all Division 09 spec items. None of it shows on a plan, and all of it is labour. It pairs directly with the drywall takeoff on the same job.

ACCESS

High work averaged in with the rest

Stairwells, atriums, high lobbies and exterior elevations need lifts, swing stages or scaffold. Rolled into one blended wall area, the access cost disappears — and it doesn't disappear on site.

FAQPainting takeoff questions

Painting takeoff FAQs

How much does a painting takeoff cost?
From $450 — the single-trade finish takeoff tier. What moves the fixed price is sheet count, how many coating systems the spec runs, how many levels there are, and how much of the set genuinely repeats. A written fixed quote comes back within 4 business hours and doesn't move afterwards. Full schedule on the takeoff pricing page.
How fast is it delivered?
Next business day on single-trade painting sets. Larger or multi-building sets get a firm delivery date with the quote. Same-day rush is +50%. If it lands after the date on the quote, you pay half.
Do you separate substrates and coating systems?
Always. Substrate decides preparation and material consumption, and the system decides coats. Gypsum, CMU, concrete, plaster, wood and metal each get their own lines, with the spec's system code and coat count written against them, so your production rates apply to surfaces that actually behave the same way.
Do you count doors and frames?
Yes — by leaf and by side, taken from the Division 08 door schedule rather than the finish plan, with factory-finished leaves marked as excluded. Hollow metal frames, borrowed lights and relites are counted separately.
What do you need besides the drawings?
The Division 09 painting spec section and the door schedule, if they aren't in the drawing set. Without the spec we can measure areas but can't attach systems, coats or preparation — and that's the part your rate is built on. Send a link through the quote form: Dropbox, Drive, WeTransfer, BuildingConnected, BidCentral, SmartBid or a plan room login all work.
Can the workbook use our own cost codes?
Yes. CSI MasterFormat is the default; send your cost code list and the workbook is built to it, so quantities drop into your estimating system without re-mapping.
What's excluded?
Pricing, unless you add price placeholders. Mechanical and electrical entirely — SharpBid doesn't take off Divisions 21 through 28, though items in those divisions that are scheduled to be painted are still quantified for you. Anything the drawings genuinely leave undetermined is listed on the assumptions page instead of buried in a quantity.
