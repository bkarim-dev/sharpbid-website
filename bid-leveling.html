Bid leveling & scope-gap analysis

Three quotes for the same package are almost never for the same scope. Bid leveling puts them on one common basis so the cheapest number is actually the cheapest job — inclusions, exclusions, qualifications and quantity assumptions compared line by line. WHOWho this is for

Who bid leveling
is for

Two situations, same discipline. In both, the problem isn't arithmetic — it's that the quotes on your desk are describing different amounts of work in the same units.

SUBCONTRACTORS

Levelling your own supplier and sub-sub quotes

You've got three material quotes and two installation quotes for one package, and each one carries its own set of exclusions. Levelling them tells you which number is genuinely lowest once the gaps are filled — and which supplier just handed you a cheap price by leaving something out that you'll carry anyway.

GENERAL CONTRACTORS

Levelling sub bids before you award

The spread between three sub bids is usually scope, not price. Levelling turns the spread into a list of specific differences — what each bidder included, excluded and qualified — so the award conversation is about the same building rather than three interpretations of it.

MATRIXWhat the deliverable shows

What the leveling
matrix shows

You get one Excel workbook with the bidders as columns and the scope as rows, so any line can be read across. Nothing is summarized away — if a bidder is silent on something, the cell says so, because silence is the finding.

ROWS

Scope broken into comparable line items

The package is decomposed into the items a bid can actually differ on — materials, assemblies, accessories, preparation, access, and the interfaces with adjacent trades. Rows come from the drawings and the specification, not from any one bidder's proposal, so nobody's format sets the agenda.

COLUMNS

One column per bidder, read across

Each bidder's position on each row: included, excluded, qualified, or not mentioned. "Not mentioned" is its own state and it's the one that costs money, because it's the item everyone assumes somebody else has.

GAPS

A gap list, with the source named

Every item nobody priced, every item priced by more than one bidder, and every place a bidder's qualification quietly shifts risk to you — each with the drawing sheet or spec section it comes from, so it can be resolved in a phone call instead of a dispute.

QUANTITIES

Quantity assumptions compared, not just prices

Where bidders state quantities, they're compared against each other and against the drawings. Two bidders pricing the same unit rate against quantities that differ by fifteen percent is a finding, and it's invisible if you only read the bottom line.

ADJUSTED

An adjusted comparison, with the maths visible

Each bidder gets an adjusted total that adds the value of what they left out, using your own numbers or a stated allowance. Every adjustment is a visible line you can argue with — the point is a defensible comparison, not a verdict handed down.

SUMMARY

A one-page summary you can take to a meeting

The ranking, the material differences behind it, and the open questions worth an RFI before award. Short enough to be read in a scope meeting, with the full matrix behind it when someone disagrees.

Change-order & claim
quantity checks

An independent remeasure of disputed quantities, charged hourly at $150 with a three-hour minimum. It's the same measurement discipline as a takeoff, pointed at a number somebody is already arguing about.

  • Independent remeasure of the disputed scopeMeasured from the drawings and the change documents, without reference to either side's figure until ours is finished.
  • Before-and-after comparisonOriginal scope quantities against revised scope quantities, so the delta is a measured difference rather than an assertion.
  • Basis of measurement stated in writingIncluding the deduction rules used, which is usually where two honest parties end up with different numbers.
  • A written summary you can attachShort, factual and traceable to sheets and details, suitable for attaching to a claim or a change-order response.

This is a quantity opinion, not a legal or contractual one. It says what the drawings measure to — not who is entitled to what under the contract.

Bid leveling FAQs

How much does bid leveling cost?
From $600. What moves the fixed price is the number of bidders, the size of the package and whether the drawings need to be measured as part of the exercise. Change-order and claim quantity checks are $150/hr with a three-hour minimum. A written fixed quote comes back within 4 business hours. Full schedule on the takeoff pricing page.
What do you need from me?
The bids or quotes themselves, the drawings and specification the package was bid from, and any addenda that were issued. If you have your own scope checklist or bid form, send that too and the matrix gets built around it. Without the drawings we can compare what bidders said to each other, but not to what the job actually requires — which is where most of the value is.
Do you recommend a winner?
We give you a ranked, adjusted comparison with every adjustment shown as a line you can argue with, plus the open questions worth an RFI before award. The decision stays yours — commercial factors, past performance and schedule risk sit outside a quantity document, and pretending otherwise would make the analysis less useful, not more.
How fast is it?
Every leveling job gets a firm delivery date with the fixed quote, sized to the number of bidders and the package. Same-day rush is +50%. As with everything else here: if it lands after the date on the quote, you pay half.
Can you level a package you also did the takeoff for?
Yes, and it's cheaper that way, since the quantities are already measured and the scope is already broken down. It's a common pairing on a retainer — the takeoff goes out at bid time, the leveling comes back when the quotes land.
Is bid leveling included in a retainer?
It's included in the Growth retainer at $2,500 a month. Starter and Partner clients can add it at the schedule price. Retainers run month-to-month and unused takeoffs roll one month — details on the pricing page.
Will you sign a confidentiality agreement?
Yes. Bidders' numbers and client drawings are never shared, which is the standing practice rather than something that needs asking for. If your own agreement needs signing, send it over.
