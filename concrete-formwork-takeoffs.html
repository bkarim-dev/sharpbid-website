How we measure
concrete and formwork

Volume and contact area are measured independently, element by element, off the structural drawings. The single most expensive mistake in this trade is pricing forming from concrete volume — a thin wall with two faces costs far more to form than a thick one with the same cubic metres.

CONTACT AREA

Formwork is measured as surface, not derived from volume

Every formed face is measured on its own. Two-sided walls, one-sided walls against shoring, column perimeters, beam sides and soffits, and slab edges all get counted as the surfaces they are. Contact area is what your forming crew is paid for, and there's no factor that gets you there from cubic metres.

BY ELEMENT

Volume grouped the way you pour

Footings, grade beams, walls, columns, slab-on-grade, suspended slabs and site work are separated, with the specified mix and strength against each. That's how the ready-mix gets ordered and how the pour gets scheduled, so that's how the workbook reports it.

S-SHEETS

Measured from the structural set, checked against architectural

Dimensions, reinforcing and element sizes come from the structural drawings. The architectural set is read alongside for finishes, site elements and anything the two disagree about — and where they do, both readings go on the assumptions page with the one we measured.

FORM TYPE

Form conditions separated, because rates differ

A straight wall form, a curved form, a one-sided form braced against shoring and an architectural liner are four different costs over the same square footage of contact area. Each is reported separately with the condition named, so your rates land where they belong.

REBAR

Reinforcing from the schedule, by size

Bars are taken from the reinforcing schedule by size and element, with mesh by area and dowels and couplers counted each. Weights are reported by size so your supplier can quote and your crew can plan placing, rather than one blended tonnage.

FINISH

Slab finishes are their own quantity

Broom, steel trowel, burnished and exposed finishes are separate line items with the specified flatness noted. Finishing is labour over area and has nothing to do with the volume underneath it.

M-SHEETS

Housekeeping pads read off the mechanical drawings

Equipment bases and housekeeping pads are drawn on the mechanical sheets and poured by you. We read the M-sheets as part of the takeoff, count them each with volume attached, and note the equipment they serve.

CYCLE

On a tower, the typical floor is measured once and extended visibly

High-rise cast-in-place lives or dies on repetition. The typical floor is measured in full — volume, contact area by form condition, reinforcing and finish — then extended across the levels it applies to, with transfer slabs, mechanical levels and the podium measured on their own. The workbook shows which levels were measured and which were extended, so you can audit the multiplier instead of trusting it.

UNITS

Metric or imperial, whichever you bid in

Structural sets in Canada are commonly metric; smaller residential and site work is often imperial. The workbook arrives in the units you price with, or both columns, with element marks carried through exactly as the structural drawings write them.

GAPSScope gaps we flagCaught before you bid

Scope gaps we flag
on concrete packages

The six that cost concrete subs money most often. Each one lands on the assumptions and exclusions page with the sheet it came from, so you can qualify it in your bid instead of meeting it in a change order.

CONTACT AREA

Formwork priced off concrete volume

Formwork is paid by contact area, not by cubic metres. A 200 mm wall with two faces costs far more to form than a 400 mm wall of the same volume. Any takeoff that gives you cubes and expects you to derive forming from them is handing you the risk.

M-SHEETS

Housekeeping pads on the mechanical drawings

They're shown with the equipment, not on the structural plans, and they're poured by you. Read the M-sheets or they're a freebie — and on a building with a full mechanical room, it's a lot of small pours with a lot of edge form.

SITE CONDITIONS

One-sided forming against shoring or rock

A one-sided form has to be braced and tied back, and it costs multiples of a conventional two-sided wall over the same contact area. Whether it applies is a site and shoring question the structural drawings don't always answer, so we flag it rather than assume.

SPEC

Slab flatness and finish written only in the spec

The finish class and any flatness tolerance are Division 03 spec items. A burnished or high-tolerance slab is a different crew, a different rate and sometimes a different pour sequence — none of which appears on a plan.

COORDINATION

Embeds and blockouts owned by other trades

Weld plates, sleeves, anchor bolts and openings come from the metals, mechanical and electrical packages but get installed by you before the pour. We count them and name where they came from, which is the same reading behind the misc metals takeoff.

JOINTS

Saw-cut and control joints left out of the number

Joint layout is lineal footage with saw time, filler and sealant attached, and on a large slab-on-grade it's a real line. It's easy to price the slab and forget the cutting, the filling and the second mobilization it usually takes.

More of these by trade in the bid traps list, and the cost side in what a construction takeoff costs in Canada.

Concrete & formwork takeoff FAQs

How much does a concrete takeoff cost?
From $500 for low-rise and small-site scopes. What moves the fixed price is how many distinct elements the structural set carries, whether reinforcing is in scope, and the amount of site concrete. High-rise cast-in-place is quoted per project rather than off the schedule. A written fixed quote comes back within 4 business hours either way. Full schedule on the takeoff pricing page.
How fast is it delivered?
Every concrete set gets a firm delivery date with the fixed quote, sized to the structural set. Same-day rush is +50%. If it lands after the date on the quote, you pay half.
Do you take off high-rise cast-in-place work?
Yes — with the right lead time and priced per project rather than off the $500 schedule. Cycle forming has its own production logic: typical-floor repetition measured once and extended visibly, contact area separated by form condition, and the shoring and reshoring cycle read properly. That takes longer than a foundation package, so send the structural set early and say it's high-rise when you ask for the quote.
Do you give formwork as contact area or as volume?
Contact area, always, measured face by face and reported separately from concrete volume. Forming is paid by the surface your crew builds and strips, and no factor gets you there reliably from cubic metres.
Is reinforcing included?
It can be. Bars are taken from the reinforcing schedule by size and element, with mesh by area and dowels and couplers counted each, and weights reported by size. Tell us at quote time whether rebar is in your scope, since it changes the fixed price.
What drawings do you need?
The structural set including reinforcing and element schedules, the architectural for finishes and site elements, and the mechanical drawings if housekeeping pads are yours. Send a link through the quote form — Dropbox, Drive, WeTransfer, BuildingConnected, BidCentral, SmartBid or a plan room login.
What's excluded?
Pricing, unless you add price placeholders. Shoring design, mix design and any structural engineering are engineering scopes, not takeoffs. Mechanical and electrical entirely — SharpBid doesn't take off Divisions 21 through 28, though blockouts and pads serving that equipment are counted. Over-pour and waste are stated separately rather than built into the volume.
