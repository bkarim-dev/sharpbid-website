How we measure
glazing

Elevations and the glass schedule are read against each other before anything is counted. On most sets they disagree somewhere — a panel shown as spandrel on the elevation and missing from the schedule is the classic — and whichever one your supplier quoted, the other one is what gets built.

ELEVATION BY ELEVATION

Measured by face, not as one building total

Each elevation is measured on its own and reported that way. North and south often carry different coatings, and every face carries different access, staging and crane time. A single building-wide square footage hides all of it.

RECONCILE

Elevations against the glass schedule, panel by panel

Every lite is matched to a makeup in the schedule. Panels shown on an elevation but absent from the schedule, and makeups scheduled but never drawn, both go onto the assumptions page with the position we measured — so it becomes an RFI instead of a claim.

GLASS VS METAL

Glass area and framing lineal come out separately

Your glass supplier quotes area and lite counts; your metal supplier quotes lineal by profile; your installers price openings. All three come out of the same takeoff as separate sections, so you're not deriving one from another with a factor.

TYPE MARK

Windows and doors follow the schedule, counted each

Windows, glazed doors, sidelites and transoms are counted by type mark against the window and door schedules, with operable units separated from fixed. Sizes come from the schedule rather than being scaled off an elevation.

JOINTS

Sealant measured by condition, in lineal feet

Perimeter sealant is measured at head, jamb, sill and at every transition to an adjacent assembly, by the joint condition. It's a line item most subs never quantify and most general contractors assume is included — which makes it free work unless it's written down.

DETAILS

Anchorage comes off the details and the structural set

Embeds, anchors and attachment points are drawn in the details and on the structural sheets, not on the elevations. They're counted each, against the condition they serve, so the attachment isn't a lump-sum guess in an otherwise precise bid.

INTERFACE

The envelope transition is named

Where the glazed wall meets cladding, roofing or the air and vapour barrier, the membrane tie-in and closure can fall on either side of the trade line. We quantify the transition and name the division the spec puts it in — the same joint we look at from the other side on roofing and envelope takeoffs.

UNITS

Metric or imperial, whichever you bid in

Commercial and institutional sets in Canada are commonly metric; residential work is often imperial. The workbook arrives in the units you price with, or both columns, with type marks and makeup codes carried through exactly as the schedule writes them.

GAPSScope gaps we flagCaught before you bid

Scope gaps we flag
on glazing packages

The six that cost glazing subs money most often. Each one lands on the assumptions and exclusions page with the sheet it came from, so you can qualify it in your bid instead of meeting it in a change order.

SCHEDULE VS ELEVATION

Spandrel drawn but never scheduled

Spandrel shows on the elevations and is missing from the glass schedule. If your glass quote followed the schedule, you just bought the spandrel — and on a tower elevation that is a very large number to absorb.

JOINTS

Perimeter sealant nobody quantified

General contractors measure it twice and subs measure it zero times. Head, jamb, sill and every transition is lineal footage with labour and access attached. Unquantified, it's free work — and on a fully glazed building it is a lot of free work.

S-SHEETS

Embeds drawn on the structural set

The curtain wall lives on the architectural elevations; the embeds that hold it live on the structural sheets. Bid one set and the anchorage is somebody's assumption — usually yours, discovered after the concrete is poured.

DIVISION LINE

Glass railings that may be Division 05

Glass guards and railings sit in Division 08 on some specs and Division 05 on others. We measure them, keep them in their own section and name which division the spec uses, so you and the metals fabricator aren't both excluding the same scope.

ENVELOPE TIE-IN

Membrane transitions at the rough opening

The air and vapour barrier has to tie into the window or curtain wall perimeter, and the spec doesn't always say whose membrane, whose flashing or whose sequence. Left ambiguous, it becomes a site problem billed to whoever is standing there.

ACCESS

Elevations averaged into one area

A ground-floor storefront and a tenth-floor replacement lite are the same square footage and nothing like the same cost. Measured as one building total, swing stage, crane and lift time all disappear from the bid and reappear on the schedule.

FAQGlazing takeoff questions

Glazing takeoff FAQs

How much does a glazing takeoff cost?
From $900. Glazing sits above the finish-trade tier because elevations, glass schedules, door and window schedules and structural details all have to be read against each other. What moves the fixed price is the number of elevations and systems, how many makeups the schedule carries, and building height. A written fixed quote comes back within 4 business hours. Full schedule on the takeoff pricing page.
How fast is it delivered?
Every glazing set gets a firm delivery date with the fixed quote, sized to the number of elevations. Same-day rush is +50%. If it lands after the date on the quote, you pay half.
Does this page cover building envelope too?
No — this page is Division 08 only: curtain wall, storefront, entrances, windows, interior glazing and the glass itself. Roofing, cladding, insulation, air and vapour barrier and rainscreen are a separate scope with its own page and its own price. See roofing and building envelope takeoffs. If you carry both, say so and they get quoted together.
Do you separate glass area from framing?
Yes. Glass comes out by makeup in area and lite counts, framing by profile in lineal feet, and openings counted each. Your glass supplier, your metal supplier and your installers all quote different things, and they shouldn't have to derive their numbers from each other.
What drawings do you need?
Elevations, the glass schedule, the window and door schedules, the glazing details and the structural sheets showing embeds. Send a link through the quote form — Dropbox, Drive, WeTransfer, BuildingConnected, BidCentral, SmartBid or a plan room login. If the structural set isn't available, we'll measure without it and flag the anchorage as excluded rather than guess.
Can the workbook use our own cost codes?
Yes. CSI MasterFormat is the default; send your cost code list and the workbook is built to it, so quantities drop into your estimating system without re-mapping.
What's excluded?
Pricing, unless you add price placeholders. Mechanical and electrical entirely — SharpBid doesn't take off Divisions 21 through 28. Thermal and structural performance calculations are an engineering scope, not a takeoff. Anything the drawings genuinely leave undetermined is listed on the assumptions page instead of buried in a quantity.
